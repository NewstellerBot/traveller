How the score works

score = e−d / 1500

d is the distance in kilometres between the guess and the true spot. A reply with no coordinates scores 0, and a model's score is its average over the 100 places.

A closer guess always scores more, and no single miss can cost more than one place. The 1,500 km scale is close to that of GeoScore, the GeoGuessr-style metric used in geolocation research.

Median error is the distance that half of a model's guesses beat.

Score against cost

Costs are what Prime Intellect's inference API charged for the run. It is the API verifiers used to call every model.

Top 5 fails

The five places where the thirteen models scored lowest on average, leaving out one whose photos came out black. On each map, × is the true spot and each dot is one model's guess.

1. Central Nigeria

A paved road across a flat plain of wet green fields. Twelve of the thirteen models read it as rice country in Asia: six said Cambodia, and the rest Thailand, Myanmar, India and Sri Lanka. Only Gemini 2.5 Flash Lite stayed in Africa, with a guess in Ghana 1,200 km away.

2. Irkutsk Oblast, Siberia

A highway junction in flat farmland with dark ploughed soil. Nine models named Russia, but six of them put it in the farm belt in the west of the country, 3,200 to 4,600 km away, and two more said Ukraine. Russia is wide enough that the right country can still score close to zero. Claude Opus 5.5 was closest, 1,400 km off in Altai Krai.

3. Outback South Australia

A gravel road across a dry, scrubby plain. Six models, among them the five highest-scoring, said South Africa's Northern Cape, and two more said Namibia, over 10,000 km away. Three models got the right state, and DeepSeek V4.1 Flash was 34 km off.

4. Santa Cruz, Bolivia

A gravel road cut into a dry, forested hillside, with no signs in view. Three models said Oaxaca in Mexico; others said Georgia, Kyrgyzstan, India, Taiwan and Chile. Only the two Claude models named Bolivia, both about 370 km from the spot.

5. Busuanga, Philippines

A red dirt lane through dense tropical bush. Six models said Cambodia and two Thailand. Inkling 2 Mini gave the coordinates of Manila, 290 km away, but with the longitude's sign flipped, which put its guess in the Pacific.

How it was run

The 100 places are spread evenly over six world regions and come from 500 random Street View locations. Each model saw every place once, at its default settings, except Gemini 4 Neon, which ran at low reasoning effort; at its default it cost about seven times as much in a 20-place trial. GPT-6.2 Astra is scored on 99 places, because the API returned an error for one place on every attempt. Claude Fable 5.2's lead holds when the models are compared place by place; Claude Opus 5.5 and GPT-6.2 Sol are too close to separate. With one photo instead of four, every model scored lower, by 0.08 on average. A reply without coordinates in the required format scores zero; that happened 7 times for Gemini 2.5 Flash Lite and once for Kimi K2.6.

Photos are from Google Street View.
